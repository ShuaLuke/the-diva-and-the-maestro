A plan for the book's website: what it is for, how it is organized, what the home page says, and how the family's photographs and scores become the site's archive.

What the site is for

One site, three readers. The pages below serve all three, but the home page is built for the first.

  • The reader deciding whether to buy. They need the story in ten seconds, proof it's good in one minute, and a buy button that works. The prologue is the proof: it is already a complete, self-contained scene.
  • The reader who has the book open. They want to see the faces, hear the songs, and check who Diamond Jim or Harry Burleigh was. The book's own back matter, the biographic summaries, becomes a reference section on the site.
  • The teacher, scholar, or descendant. They want the archive: the photographs, the scores, the record of what happened. This is the part of the site that will still be visited in twenty years.

Positioning

Say "novel" plainly and early. The book is a novel based on a true story, written by the family. That is a strength, not a caveat. It lets the site promise scenes, dialogue, and feeling that a biography cannot, and it separates the book cleanly from Marva Griffin Carter's Swing Along, the academic biography of Cook. Readers who know that book are the likeliest early buyers, so the Book page should answer "how is this different?" in two sentences.

Lead with the box. Hundreds of pages found in a cardboard box after Mercer Cook's death, a grandson who barely knew his grandparents, a story nobody had told. That is the site's origin myth and its most shareable fact. It deserves its own page and a paragraph on the home page.

Abbie first. The title puts the Diva ahead of the Maestro, and the prologue is told largely through her eyes. The site should follow: her page comes before his in the navigation, and her voice opens the home page.

Be as frank as the book is. Abbie was fourteen at the audition. The prologue handles this head-on, including Will Marion's own discomfort. The site copy should match that stance exactly, neither hiding the age nor sensationalizing it. Whatever sentence the authors settle on for the Diva page will be quoted, so it is worth writing carefully once.

The archive: photographs and scores

This is where the site outlives the launch. A few decisions up front save a great deal of rework later.

One page per item

Every photograph and every score gets its own page with a stable address, a caption, a date (or "circa"), the people in it, the source, and the rights line. Search engines index these individually. A gallery that is one long scrolling page does not get found.

Scores can be shown in full

Anything published in the United States before 1931 is in the public domain in 2026. That covers Darktown Is Out Tonight (1898), Love in a Cottage, Swing Along (1902), the In Dahomey songs, and I'm Coming Virginia (1926). Full scans can be posted and downloaded. Manuscripts and unpublished material follow the family's wishes.

Photographs need a rights line

For each image, note whether it is from the family collection, a library, or a newspaper. Family photographs can be shown freely. Archive images may need a credit line or permission even when old. Record this once, in a spreadsheet, before scanning.

Scan once, at high resolution

Scan at 600 dpi for scores and 400 dpi or better for photographs, save the masters as TIFF, and let the site generate its own smaller copies. The masters are the family's, whatever happens to the website.

Listen where you can

Each score page should link to a recording if one exists: period recordings on archive.org and the Library of Congress, modern performances on YouTube. The site should not host audio it does not own.

Start with twenty

Launch with the twenty best items fully captioned rather than two hundred with no captions. Add the rest over time. Each new batch is a newsletter issue.

How to build it

A static site, published on Vercel. The whole site is text and images, so it needs no database and no server. A static site is fast, nearly free to host, and will still work in a decade. Astro is the right tool: each page and each archive item is a plain text file with a picture beside it, and the site is regenerated when anything changes.

The newsletter lives with a mailing service, not on the site. Buttondown or Mailchimp handle signups, sending, and unsubscribes. The site only shows the form.

Domain. The book's title order is Diva first, so the domain should be too. I can check availability for thedivaandthemaestro.com and short alternatives when you're ready.

Who updates it. Adding an archive item should be as simple as dropping an image and a caption file into a folder. I will set it up that way and write a one-page guide.

Things to settle

  1. Publisher and date. The preface is dated August 2026. Is there a publisher, a release date, and a pre-order link? This decides whether the home page button says "Buy" or "Pre-order."
  2. The sentence about Abbie's age. Write it once for the Diva page, in the book's voice, and use it everywhere.
  3. Cover art. The site needs the final cover at full resolution, plus the two best portraits for the hero.
  4. The first twenty archive items. Which photographs and scores, with dates and sources.
  5. Author photos and bios. One paragraph and one photograph each.
  6. Chapter 15. The table of contents reads "Flies the Coup." The idiom is "flies the coop." Worth catching before the title appears on the site or the book.
